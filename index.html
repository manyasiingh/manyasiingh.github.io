About

I'm an MSc IT graduate at Dhirubhai Ambani University (DAU), working as a research intern at the Speech Research Lab, where I study how dysarthric speech can be represented in ways a clinician can actually interpret, from articulatory information in self-supervised models to biomarkers read directly off phoneme-posterior alignment paths. Most of my work sits at the intersection of clinical speech-language pathology and representation learning: building features that a speech-language pathologist could actually use, not just a benchmark number.

Alongside this, I probe what general-purpose pretrained models do and don't encode, from steganographic signal in frozen vision encoders to reproducing recent activation-steering work for multilingual LLM reasoning and tracking down the bugs that reproducibility studies tend to surface. Before DAU, I completed my BSc in Data Science at Navrachna University.

Hidden in the Pixels, Lost in the Embedding? Cross-Architecture Probing of Steganographic Detectability
ICVGIP 2026 · Kolkata, India
Manya Singh, Avantika Agarwal, Hemant A. Patil
Accepted

Linear-probed five frozen vision encoders (CLIP ViT-B/32 and ViT-L/14, SigLIP, DINOv2, ResNet-50) for implicit sensitivity to LSB replacement and LSB matching, with a Gaussian-noise control and SPAM / chi-square classical baselines. Embedding shift tracks perturbation magnitude, not message structure, while detectability varies with training objective (AUC 0.99–1.00 for language-aligned encoders vs. 0.62 for DINOv2 at 4-bit full payload); all encoders stay near chance at realistic 1-bit payloads. Replicated on CIFAR-10 using leakage-free grouped cross-validation.

Interpretable Dysarthria Representation from PPG-DTW Paths

“Beyond Phone Durations: Interpretable Dysarthria Representation from PPG-DTW Paths”

A novel approach that treats the frame-level DTW path over wav2vec2 phonetic posteriorgrams as the representation itself, deriving five interpretable temporal, topological, and phonetic biomarkers for dysarthria on the UA-Speech corpus. Achieved AUC 0.952, competitive with a 1024-d wav2vec2 embedding (0.970) and above an MFCC-SVM baseline (0.917), and showed the non-duration biomarkers retain duration-independent signal after controlling for stretch ratio. Against the Montreal Forced Aligner, duration agrees at the speaker level (r = 0.95) with a larger group-separation effect (d = 1.25 vs. 0.74).
